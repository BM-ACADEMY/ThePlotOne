# PART 1 — THE STRUCTURAL DECISION

**One plan \= one campaign \= one project.** Slots and bundles are gone.

| Concept | Definition | Cost |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Listing** | A property sitting on the platform — visible in search, matched to requirements | **Free** |
| **Campaign** | A project we actively advertise with committed lead delivery | **Paid** |

> **"List everything free. Advertise what you want to sell now."**

**Why:** a pooled plan forces us to divide leads between projects, and any division we choose feels wrong to the promoter whose layout got less. Per-campaign commitments make that argument structurally impossible. Each project has a number and either hits it or doesn't.

# PART 4 — LEAD DISTRIBUTION LOGIC

## 4.1 The governing rule

> **Whoever paid for the lead owns it.**

A lead carrying a `campaignId` **can only ever match that campaign's project.** Enforce in the data layer, not business logic. If a promoter learns his paid campaign produced a buyer shown someone else's property, the business is finished in that town.

## 4.3 When several campaigns match

**Different promoters:**

1. Highest plan tier (Pro \> Growth \> Starter)  
2. Most unsold inventory  
3. Rotation pointer

**Same promoter, multiple campaigns** — this is the new case:

1. **Better fit** — budget band and area accuracy  
2. **Campaign behind pace gets it** ← self-balancing; a lagging campaign pulls ambiguous leads so both finish on target  
3. Closer to expiry  
4. **Deliver ONCE, tagged to one campaign. Never double-count.**

He sees the buyer's full requirement anyway and can show him any of his layouts.

## 4.4 Campaign as an independent contract

Each campaign has its **own** budget, committed minimum, go-live date, expiry, pacing, reporting, extension clock and renewal. **Nothing pools between campaigns.** One itemised invoice per account.

**Align cycles option:** at signup of a second campaign, offer pro-rating so both renew on the same date. Turns two renewal conversations into one.

## 4.5 Pacing per campaign

daily target \= remaining committed ÷ days remaining

behind by 15%  → raise daily budget within plan limits

ahead by 30%   → lower budget, protect margin

ceiling reached → pause, COMPLETED\_EARLY, renewal offer

CPL \> ₹400 for 3 days → alert admin \+ message promoter with the choice

## 4.6 What we never promise

- Any lead count on an unadvertised listing  
- Equal leads across a promoter's campaigns (each has its own contract instead)  
- Enquiries per agent listing  
- Sales, closures or site visit numbers

# PART 5 — LISTING QUALITY SCORE

A listing with views and no enquiries needs an explanation, not a complaint.

Shown in the owner's panel: views · enquiries · **conversion vs area average** · specific issues — *"priced 18% above area average"* · *"only 2 photos, listings with 8+ get 3× enquiries"* · *"no DTCP number added"*.

Turns a zero-enquiry listing from our failure into his to-do list, and it drives Boost purchases.

# PART 7 — KNOWN TRADE-OFFS (accepted deliberately)

| Trade-off | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- |
| Smaller first sale (₹24,999 vs ₹39,999) | Volume ladder; more promoters beats bigger deals at this stage |
| Capacity fills at fewer promoters | 25–30 campaign cap is honest; raise only with staff |
| Fixed cost per small campaign | 2nd campaign must be Growth+ |
| More renewal dates | Align-cycles option at signup |
| No buffer to hide under-delivery | Accepted — visible delivery is the point |
| Self-competition between his campaigns | Overlap flag before launch |

**Why this is right at this stage:** we have no closed deals and no case study, in a market where promoters talk daily. One delivery dispute travels the district in a week. This model makes that dispute structurally impossible. Being un-arguable is worth more than being optimal.
